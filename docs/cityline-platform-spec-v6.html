01Summary

Cityline Airport Transfers gets most of its work from partner platforms such as Trip.com, which take about 18% commission, and wants a strong commission-free direct channel. This document specifies one Next.js application, self-hosted on a single IONOS VPS with PostgreSQL, Caddy and a background worker in Docker. It has two parts: a public website with hundreds of SEO pages and a fixed-price booking engine, and an operations admin that records every job from every source, together with drivers, vehicles, costs and profit. Drivers get their jobs automatically on WhatsApp. The domain and existing IONOS plan are already set up (§11) and keep the domain, DNS, mailboxes and backup copies; the VPS runs everything else (§10). A staging site on the same server and a launch gate keep work away from customers until go-live (§12). The build order is website and booking first, admin panel last (§15). The development plan is in §15.

Scope rules

  • One job register for all sources. Website bookings create jobs automatically. Supplier, phone and other jobs are created by staff. Every job records where it came from, and the same job can't be entered twice (§2).
  • Staff update job progress by hand. No live location, no driver app, no GPS tracking, no automatic driver allocation, no automatic flight tracking.
  • Assigning a driver sends a WhatsApp message automatically. Changes, reassignments and cancellations are sent automatically too.
  • The admin keeps the finance records: each job's revenue, commission, driver pay and costs; company expenses; driver statements; money owed by suppliers; profit reports. Driver pay rules, whether pay appears in WhatsApp messages, and supplier commission rates are settings you can change at any time.
  • Hosting: everything runs on one IONOS VPS (about £18/month on top of the existing plan). You own server maintenance in exchange (§12).
  • Admin last: the website and booking flow are built first. §15 explains the record-keeping gap this creates and the two ways to cover it.
  • Design, logos and photos come from Cityline's Figma file (or Google Stitch). No palette or typography is specified here (§6).

How conflicts between sources are prevented

  • One jobs table and one reference format for every source (e.g. J-004271). Website jobs also carry the booking reference (CL-7K4Q2P).
  • Source is required and can't be changed after creation: website, supplier, phone, whatsapp, email, account or other.
  • The staff form can't create website jobs. Only the checkout process can, so a website booking can't be entered by hand as well.
  • Website jobs are locked: the price, the customer's details and the route come from the booking. Staff change them through "Amend booking", which reprices, charges or refunds, and emails the customer. Driver, vehicle, status and internal notes can be edited freely.
  • Supplier jobs are checked for duplicates: the database won't accept the same supplier reference twice for the same supplier.
  • Possible-duplicate warning: if a new job matches an existing one on pickup time (±30 min), passenger surname or flight number, the admin warns before saving.

06Design sources

There is no design system in this document. The visual design comes from Cityline's own files, and the build follows them:

  • Figma file (to be supplied). Claude reads it directly through the Figma MCP connection, or from Dev Mode exports, and builds React components from the frames. Brand colours, typography, spacing and component states all come from there.
  • Google Stitch may be used to generate or refine screens; its output feeds the same component build.
  • What this document still fixes, because it isn't a styling matter: the pages that must exist (§5), what each page must contain and do (§8), the rules the booking flow must obey (§7), and accessibility and performance targets (§9).

For the design files to be buildable, they need to cover: the quote widget in its three uses (hero, inline on landing pages, compact sticky bar), the four booking steps with the running total, the confirmation page, manage-booking, the home page, one route page, one airport and terminal page, fleet and service pages, an information/policy page, contact, 404, plus the shared header, footer, cookie banner, form fields, buttons, alerts and empty states. Mobile (390px) and desktop (1440px) for each, and the error states listed in §7 (price changed, quote expired, payment failed, pickup too soon).

Two constraints the design must respect, whatever it looks like: no image or wording that implies a licensed London taxi (CMP-01), and the footer legal block from CMP-09. Accessibility target: WCAG 2.2 AA, so colour choices need enough contrast and every control needs a visible focus state.

07Business logic

Catalogue (editable in the admin)

  • Service types: airport, cruise port, station, point-to-point, hourly, tours, events, weddings, minibus/group.
  • Vehicle classes: capacity, example models, photos, EV flag, order on the page.
  • Places: airports → terminals, ports, stations, hotels, areas, zones (by postcode district or map area).
  • Extras: child seats, extra stop, meet & greet, extra luggage, pet, wheelchair accessible.
  • Offers: promo codes, return discount, perks for booking direct, seasonal banners, tour packages.
  • Suppliers: name, default commission %, payment terms, contact, dashboard URL.

Pricing engine (website quotes)

StepRule
1 · Base fareA fixed route fare if one matches (place/zone ↔ place/zone, per class). Otherwise a distance tariff (flag fall + £/mile bands + £/min) with a minimum fare.
2 · Hourly£/hour per class, with minimum hours and an included-miles allowance.
3 · Location chargesAirport pickup and drop-off fees, parking, congestion charge.
4 · Time modifiersNight, bank holiday and peak-date surcharges.
5 · ExtrasSum of the selected extras.
6 · DiscountsReturn discount, then a promo code.
7 · Rounding & VATRound to £1. VAT shown only if you're registered.

The same engine suggests a price when staff create a phone or other direct job. Staff can override it, and the override is logged.

13Data safety & backups

Data is lost on a deployment when it lives inside something that gets rebuilt. Here the database and the uploaded files sit in Docker volumes that deployments never replace; only the application containers change.

IDRule
DATA-01Postgres data lives only in the named volume pg_data. The app containers hold no data.
DATA-02All uploads (media, driver and vehicle documents, receipts, generated PDFs) live in the storage volume, outside the app image.
DATA-03Schema changes happen only through committed, reviewed migrations. Reset and drop commands are blocked outside local development.
DATA-04Migrations only add things. Removing or renaming a column takes two deployments: add and copy first, drop later.
DATA-05Never run docker compose down -v or docker volume prune on the server. A server runbook says so, and only named users have shell access.
DATA-06Continuous WAL archiving (WAL-G or pgBackRest) off the server, so the database can be restored to any point within the last 15 minutes.
DATA-07A nightly encrypted pg_dump, kept 14 daily, 8 weekly and 12 monthly, copied to IONOS HiDrive and to storage outside the IONOS account.
DATA-08The storage volume is synced off-server nightly. IONOS Cloud Backup or snapshots of the whole VPS run daily and before OS upgrades.
DATA-09A restore is tested monthly into staging, with the result recorded. A backup that has never been restored doesn't count.
DATA-10Backups are held with a different provider from the VPS, so losing the IONOS account doesn't lose the data.
DATA-11Jobs and finance records are never permanently deleted. They're archived, and kept at least 12 months (TfL) and 6 years (accounting).
DATA-12Stripe and WhatsApp hold their own records of payments and messages, which can be used to rebuild if needed.

14Database design

PostgreSQL 17 on the VPS. Tables are Payload collections unless noted; Payload adds id, created_at and updated_at. Money is stored as integer pence and percentages as basis points. Times are timestamptz in UTC, displayed in Europe/London. Lists and structured data use JSONB. Case-insensitive text (emails, promo codes) uses citext or a lower() unique index. Free-text search uses a GIN trigram index. Nothing that feeds finance or the TfL register is ever permanently deleted; archived records get an archived_at date.

15Development plan

Order follows Cityline's decision: the website and booking flow first, the admin panel last. Sprints are one week for one developer working with Claude; the durations are estimates, and content (prices, copy, photos) plus third-party approvals are the usual causes of delay.

The cost of leaving the admin until last

From the day the website takes its first booking until the admin exists, there is no screen for assigning a driver, recording who dispatched a job, or keeping supplier jobs in one place. TfL requires a booking record that includes the driver's licence number, the vehicle registration and who dispatched the job (CMP-03). Two ways to cover the gap:

  • Keep the launch gate on until the admin's first slice (S8) is done, so the website isn't taking public bookings yet. Cleanest.
  • Or launch earlier and run dispatch by hand (WhatsApp plus a spreadsheet), with the website emailing every booking to staff and providing a CSV export of its own booking records. Legal, but the dispatch half of the record lives outside the system, and someone must keep it complete.

A small mitigation is built in S7: a read-only job list and CSV export, so bookings are at least visible and exportable before the full admin arrives.

17Open decisions

  • Launch before the admin exists? Either hold the launch gate until Sprint 8, or launch at Sprint 7 and run dispatch by hand for a few weeks (§15).
  • Customer accounts in v1? Recommended: guest booking plus magic-link manage-booking only, with accounts in phase 2. Fewer things to build and it converts better, and no URLs change later.
  • Published fixed fares for every route, or quote-on-request beyond a distance? This decides whether route pages can be templated at all.
  • Are supplier (Trip.com/OTA) jobs visible anywhere customer-facing? Recommended: admin only.
  • Main address: without www (recommended) or with.
  • Payment policy: pay in full at booking, or save the card and charge nearer the time? Allow cash to the driver?
  • Policies: free waiting minutes, cancellation windows and refund percentages, child-seat prices, surcharges, minimum notice.
  • Service hours: only claim 24/7 if phones are staffed 24/7.
  • VAT status, which decides how prices are displayed.
  • Staging on the same VPS? Recommended yes — it costs nothing extra and the 8 GB machine can run it.

Settings that can be changed at any time in the admin, so they don't block the build: driver pay model, whether pay appears in WhatsApp messages, supplier list and commission rates, promotions, and page content.
